# How the pictures look

Learned over two days with a non-technical owner, about a dozen rejected pictures, and the
one that worked. Each rule fixed something that made the owner lost.

## What worked

- **One picture, read top to bottom.** The simple version on top, the full system under it.
  Scrolling down is fine; going sideways, between pages or between pictures is not.
- **A map with rows by owner.** The owner's side, the shared middle, the other side, where
  things end up. Two sides that meet only in a shared place should look exactly like that.
- **The full system is the same map, opened up.** Each part is a plain white box holding its
  steps in order: filled boxes, centred words, tidy columns running down, room between them.
- **Colour by who does it**, the same colours on every level, with a key at the top.
- **Status you can see:** solid built, dashed next build, grey (fill, dotted line, words)
  later. The key says so in words.
- **Guesses yellow, with the number of the question** that settles them.
- **Arrows say what travels**, 2 to 4 words, in open space between rows.
- **Big enough to read where it opens:** text 16 px or more, about 1160 px wide, so it opens
  near 100% and the owner scrolls rather than zooms.
- **Questions only someone else can answer**, in big text (22 to 24 px) beside the simple
  version, as long as the picture still opens near full size.

## What made the owner lost

- Many separate pages or pictures (a page per flow, per level, per question).
- Cards: storyboards of 16 to 28 equal panels, each with an icon and a caption. "Storyboard"
  from an owner usually means a story, not panels.
- Wide boards that open tiny; detail that needs zooming.
- Words on everything: captions in every box, explanations on the picture, "Flow 1" labels.
- One flow lit up with the rest faded: it did not help.
- A plain chain of steps for a whole system ("too linear").
- Lanes for every layer (a full service blueprint): "too noisy".
- New looks offered when the owner could not say what was off. Ask one yes-or-no about one
  small change instead; keep everything else.
- Problems and to-do lists drawn in the same boxes as the system. They go in chat.
- The agent choosing a look without showing the owner early.

## Words on the picture

- The owner's words: real names of people, places and folders; "my system", not "bot",
  if that is how they say it.
- Most boxes need 3 to 6 words. Real numbers beat vague ones ("every minute", "3 tries").
- Code names, file paths and technical choices stay off the picture.

## Before showing it

Render it and look: nothing overlapping, no arrow through a box or through words, no label
on a box, nothing cut off. When redoing a layout, compare every label before and after so
nothing is lost. Give the picture's link in every reply that changes it.
